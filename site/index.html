The problem

You didn't buy 8,000 tracks so you could play the same 60.

Every DJ with a large library has the same failure mode. You know the top of your collection cold and the rest of it not at all. Rekordbox's own tools don't fix that: sorting by key gives you hundreds of technically-valid options with no sense of which ones hit the same, and sorting by BPM ignores key entirely. So you fall back on memory, and memory only holds the tracks you already played last month.

Key alone is too coarse

Six hundred tracks in 8A is not a shortlist. Harmonic compatibility tells you what can work, never what should.

Energy isn't a number you have

Rekordbox has no honest measure of how hard a track hits. So the set you build on paper collapses the moment you play it.

Your back catalogue is dead weight

Tracks you bought, tagged, and never opened again. The whole point of a library is that you can reach all of it.

How it works

Three steps, and you never leave your own hard drive.

Track2Mix doesn't ask you to upload your collection, sign in, or move your files. It reads the XML that Rekordbox already exports, decodes the audio sitting on your disk, and writes its findings to a single SQLite file next to it.

Export your collection

In Rekordbox: File → Export Collection in xml format. That file is the only thing Track2Mix needs to find your library.

Let it listen

Track2Mix decodes every track — MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, M4A — and pulls out loudness, dynamics, brightness, spectral movement and onset density, in parallel, across all your cores.

Dig, then export

Click any track to get its harmonically and rhythmically compatible neighbours, ranked. Tick the ones you want and export an M3U8 that Rekordbox imports directly.

# Prefer the shell? The analyzer is a standalone CLI.
track2mix analyze --xml ~/rekordbox.xml --skip-analyzed
track2mix top --by energy --genre liquid --limit 20
track2mix compat "the track you're opening with" --bpm-tol 3

What you get

Built for people with more tracks than they can remember.

A real energy score

Every track gets a 0–10 energy value derived from its actual waveform — loudness, dynamic range, brightness, spectral movement and onset density. Sort by it and your hardest tracks rise to the top.

Camelot-aware neighbours

Pick an anchor and Track2Mix returns same-key, ±1-on-the-wheel and relative major/minor matches inside your BPM tolerance. It reads Camelot, classical and open-key notation from your existing tags.

Offline by construction

No account, no upload, no telemetry. Your library never touches a server, because there is no server. Everything lives in one SQLite file you own and can delete.

Fast enough to actually run

Written in Rust, parallelised across every core, and it samples three thirty-second windows instead of grinding whole files — enough to characterise a track at a fraction of the cost.

Round-trips into Rekordbox

Exports standard M3U8 playlists that Rekordbox imports without complaint. Track2Mix is a lens on your library, not a replacement for it — nothing to migrate, nothing to lose.

GUI and CLI, same engine

A desktop app for digging and a scriptable command-line analyzer for everything else. Both read the same database, so you can automate the boring parts.

No overclaiming

What Track2Mix does, and what it deliberately doesn't.

Plenty of tools in this space promise AI that builds your set for you. Track2Mix doesn't, because that isn't a solved problem and pretending otherwise wastes your time. Here is the actual scope, so you can decide in thirty seconds whether it's useful to you.

Try it on your own library — free
  • Measures energy from the audio itself. Not from genre tags or guesswork — from loudness, dynamics, brightness, spectral flux and onset rate.
  • Reads the keys you already trust. Track2Mix uses Rekordbox's own key analysis rather than second-guessing it, so your results match what's on your CDJs.
  • Handles real, messy libraries. Moved drives can be remapped with a path map; Beatport streaming stubs and dead entries are reported instead of silently dropped.
  • Does not detect key itself. If a track has no key in Rekordbox, Track2Mix has none either. Analyse in Rekordbox first.
  • Does not write back to your Rekordbox collection. It exports playlists. Your collection file is never modified.
  • Does not build the set for you. It narrows eight thousand tracks to a handful worth auditioning. The selection is still yours.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Does this touch my Rekordbox collection?

No. Track2Mix only reads the XML file you export, and it only ever writes M3U8 playlists plus its own SQLite database. Your collection file and your audio files are never modified.

How long does analysis take?

It depends on your machine and your library, and it's parallelised across all your cores. Because Track2Mix samples three thirty-second windows per track rather than decoding whole files, it's dramatically cheaper than a full Rekordbox re-analysis. Use --dry-run first to see how many of your tracks actually resolve to files on disk before committing to a long run.

Why does it need keys from Rekordbox instead of detecting its own?

Because you already trust Rekordbox's keys and they're what show up on your CDJs. Adding a second, disagreeing key detector would create confusion rather than value. If a track is unanalysed in Rekordbox, analyse it there first.

Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ?

Not yet — Track2Mix reads the Rekordbox XML format today. The analysis engine is library-agnostic, so other sources are a matter of adding parsers. If you want one, say so in the signup form and it moves up the list.

Does my music or data get uploaded anywhere?

No. There is no backend. Analysis runs locally, results are stored locally, and the app makes no network calls to run. The only thing that ever reaches me is an email address, if you choose to give one.
