The problem

You didn't buy 8,000 tracks so you could play the same 60.

Every DJ with a large library has the same failure mode. You know the top of your collection cold and the rest of it not at all. Rekordbox's own tools don't fix that: sorting by key gives you hundreds of technically-valid options with no sense of which ones hit the same, and sorting by BPM ignores key entirely. So you fall back on memory, and memory only holds the tracks you already played last month.

Key alone is too coarse

Six hundred tracks in 8A is not a shortlist. Harmonic compatibility tells you what can work, never what should.

Energy isn't a number you have

Rekordbox has no honest measure of how hard a track hits. So the set you build on paper collapses the moment you play it.

Your back catalogue is dead weight

Tracks you bought, tagged, and never opened again. The whole point of a library is that you can reach all of it.

How it works

Three steps, and you never leave your own hard drive.

Track2Mix doesn't ask you to upload your collection, sign in, or move your files. It reads the XML that Rekordbox already exports, decodes the audio sitting on your disk, and writes its findings to a single SQLite file next to it.

Export your collection

In Rekordbox: File → Export Collection in xml format. That file is the only thing Track2Mix needs to find your library.

Let it listen

Track2Mix decodes every track — MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, M4A — and measures loudness, dynamics, brightness, spectral movement and rhythmic density, in parallel, across all your cores.

Dig, then export

Click any track to get its harmonically and rhythmically compatible neighbours, ranked. Tick the ones you want and export an M3U8 that Rekordbox imports directly.

# Prefer the shell? The analyzer is a standalone CLI.
track2mix analyze --xml ~/rekordbox.xml --skip-analyzed
track2mix top --by energy --genre liquid --limit 20
track2mix compat "the track you're opening with" --bpm-tol 3

Under the hood

It listens to the record, not the metadata.

Genre tags and star ratings describe what you thought about a track the day you filed it. Track2Mix ignores all of that and decodes the actual audio, because the only thing that reliably predicts whether two records sit together is what's in the waveform.

  • Loudness and dynamic range. How hard it hits, and how much it breathes. A compressed roller and a track that swings between silence and full weight can average out the same — telling them apart is most of the job.
  • Spectral brightness. Where the weight of the sound actually sits. This is what separates something dark and sub-heavy from something crisp and forward, long before you'd call it a genre.
  • Spectral movement. How much the sound changes moment to moment. A track that evolves and a track that loops can share every other number and feel nothing alike in a mix.
  • Rhythmic density. How busy the percussion is, independent of tempo. Two tracks at the same BPM can be miles apart on this, and it's usually what makes a transition feel rushed or flat.

Those signals are combined into one energy value per track, weighted so the result matches how a set actually feels rather than what a meter says. Getting that balance right is the part I've spent the most time on, and it's what the beta is for.

It doesn't grind through whole files

Decoding every second of every track would make a big library an overnight job. Track2Mix samples a handful of representative stretches across each record instead — enough to characterise it, at a fraction of the cost, across every core you have.

Energy is the tiebreaker, not the whole answer

Harmonic and tempo compatibility decide which tracks are eligible. Energy decides the order. That's the part Rekordbox can't do for you — turning six hundred technically-valid options into five worth auditioning.

Built to be corrected

The weighting is tuned by ear on real libraries, which means it can be wrong on yours. Every score is visible and sortable, so you can check it against tracks you know cold — and tell me where it's off.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Does this touch my Rekordbox collection?

No. Track2Mix only reads the XML file you export, and it only ever writes M3U8 playlists plus its own SQLite database. Your collection file and your audio files are never modified.

How long does analysis take?

It depends on your machine and your library, and it's parallelised across all your cores. Because Track2Mix samples representative stretches of each track rather than decoding whole files, it's dramatically cheaper than a full Rekordbox re-analysis. Use --dry-run first to see how many of your tracks actually resolve to files on disk before committing to a long run.

Why does it need keys from Rekordbox instead of detecting its own?

Because you already trust Rekordbox's keys and they're what show up on your CDJs. Adding a second, disagreeing key detector would create confusion rather than value. If a track is unanalysed in Rekordbox, analyse it there first.

Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ?

Not yet — Track2Mix reads the Rekordbox XML format today. The analysis engine is library-agnostic, so other sources are a matter of adding parsers. If you want one, say so in the signup form and it moves up the list.
